# Concept and Blueprint Hypotheses

Status: open research surface, not a frozen representation.

## Constraint

The model input is always an ordinary natural-language mathematical statement.
It never receives Lean, proof states, tactic names, Mathlib identifiers, or a
second modality. Any concepts or proof moves must be inferred from the statement
and carried in the weights.

## What “encoding concepts” can mean

We do not need to invent a compact formal proof language. We can supervise two
ordinary-language layers:

1. **Mathematical concepts**: objects and relationships such as parity,
   divisibility, modular arithmetic, factorization, order, continuity,
   invariants, symmetry, and extremality.
2. **Proof moves**: reusable actions such as choose a representation, expand a
   definition, split into exhaustive cases, construct a witness, reduce modulo
   an integer, factor an expression, apply an invariant, strengthen the
   induction hypothesis, derive a contradiction, and check an equality case.

Concept labels are seed metadata or auxiliary output targets. They are never
required input at inference time. A label is useful only if ablation shows that
it improves held-out mathematical behavior.

## Candidate natural-language target

The least novel representation is a short proof blueprint with optional
headings:

```text
Relevant ideas: parity; consecutive integers; divisibility.
Plan: Show the product contains both a factor of 2 and a factor of 3.
Key steps: Among three consecutive integers one is divisible by 3. At least one
of any two consecutive integers is even. Since 2 and 3 are coprime, their product
6 divides the original product.
Checks: The argument covers negative and zero integers as well as positive ones.
Conclusion: The product is divisible by 6.
```

This is formatted natural language, not a new proof calculus. Headings can be
removed without changing the mathematical content.

## Comparisons worth measuring

- Complete reference proof as the target.
- Concise blueprint without explicit concept labels.
- Concise blueprint with concepts and proof moves generated by the model.
- Auxiliary concept/definition examples mixed with problem-to-proof examples.
- Corrupted or irrelevant labels as a negative control.
- No post-training and answer-only post-training as controls.

The representation should be chosen by actual correctness, held-out transfer,
formalizer uplift when a fixed formalizer exists, latency, and model size—not by
how elegant the schema looks.

## Definition knowledge

Definitions should not be memorized as isolated glossary entries alone. Useful
training examples connect each definition to:

- equivalent characterizations;
- immediate consequences and counterexamples;
- common representations;
- conditions under which a proof move becomes valid;
- nearby concepts that are easy to confuse.

The current NaturalProofs definition view is source material for this layer, not
yet a trusted concept graph.
